12 questions to ask your child

Ask them out loud in 15 minutes. Younger kids can answer with a drawing. The answers that make them smile point to goals they will actually want to work on.

  1. What made you really happy this year?
  2. What do you want to learn to do by yourself?
  3. Which sport or game would you like to try?
  4. Who would you like to spend more time with?
  5. What is something you are a little scared of but want to try?
  6. Where would you love to go?
  7. What would you like to be better at in school?
  8. What do you want to make or build?
  9. How can you help at home or at school?
  10. What would you save money for?
  11. What would make you proud of yourself next year?
  12. If you could pick only three goals, which would they be?

Make a kids vision board together in 45 minutes

  1. 10 minutes: talk and dream. Sit together with paper and ask what they want to do, learn or try next year. Write every idea down, no judging.
  2. 10 minutes: answer the questions. Go through the 12 questions below out loud. Short answers are fine; younger kids can draw instead.
  3. 10 minutes: pick three goals. Choose three together: one for school, one for fun, one for being kind or brave. Give each a first step.
  4. 15 minutes: make it visible. Turn each goal into a picture card to print or a tablet lock screen, and hang the board where your child sees it every morning.

Questions

What should kids put on a vision board?

Goals they chose themselves: things to learn, sports to try, friends and family, brave and kind acts, and something to save for. This page has 36 kid-friendly goals in 6 groups (school and learning, healthy body, friends and family, creative and fun, brave and kind, money and dreams), each with a first step for this week.

What age is a vision board good for?

From about age 5 with pictures and parent help, and on their own from about age 8. Teens can use the same goals with their own words.

How do I make a vision board with my child?

Talk first, then pick three goals, write each with one small first step, and put the board where your child sees it every day, like the fridge or bedroom door.

Can teachers use this in class?

Yes. Each child picks goals from the list, makes a card in the free maker and prints it. It works well for New Year or back-to-school lessons.
